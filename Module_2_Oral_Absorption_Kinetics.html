Oral Absorption Kinetics

How dose, ka, and K shape Cmax, tmax, and AUC after oral administration, the method of residuals, and — finally — flip-flop kinetics.

Theory

One-compartment model with first-order absorption

C plasma concentration at time t (mg/L)
F bioavailability \u2014 fraction of dose absorbed
X0 administered dose (mg)
ka absorption rate constant (h-1)
K elimination rate constant (h-1)
V (Vd) volume of distribution (L)
t time after administration (h)

AUC is independent of ka and tmax. Vd and Cl are always the independent inputs; K is derived as Cl/Vd. The equation is invalid if ka = K exactly (limiting form used instead).

Normal vs. flip-flop kinetics

Normal kinetics: ka ≫ K — absorption is fast, elimination is rate-limiting, and the terminal log-linear slope reflects K.

Flip-flop kinetics: ka ≪ K — absorption becomes rate-limiting, and the terminal slope instead reflects ka.

Diagnosis: compare the oral terminal slope against an IV bolus reference line of true slope −K — parallel slopes confirm normal kinetics; a shallower oral slope indicates flip-flop.

Same 250 mg dose, Vd = 25 L, Cl = 2.625 L/h (K = 0.105 h⁻¹) for both routes. Oral F = 0.5, ka = 0.85 h⁻¹ (≫K).
