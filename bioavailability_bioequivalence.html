Bioavailability & Bioequivalence

Theory

Bioavailability (F) describes what fraction of an administered dose reaches the systemic circulation intact, and how fast. Two comparisons matter in practice: absolute bioavailability (a non-IV route compared against an IV dose of the same drug, where F is defined as 1) and relative bioavailability (two non-IV formulations of the same drug compared against each other — the basis of bioequivalence testing).

Absolute Bioavailability

Because AUC = F·Dose/Cl for any route, and clearance is a property of the drug and patient rather than the route of administration, Cl cancels when comparing two routes of the same drug in the same patient:

An IV bolus dose is by definition 100% bioavailable (FIV = 1), so this is really asking: how much smaller is the oral AUC than the IV AUC would have been at that same dose? When the two doses happen to be equal, the dose terms cancel entirely and F is simply the ratio of the two areas:

When doses differ (oral doses are often larger than IV, precisely because of the first-pass loss F is measuring), the areas must be dose-normalized (AUC/Dose) before comparing — a bigger raw oral AUC does not by itself mean better bioavailability if a bigger dose caused it.

Relative Bioavailability & Bioequivalence

Relative bioavailability compares two non-IV formulations of the same drug directly — no IV reference needed:

Bioequivalence (BE) is a regulatory judgment, not just a calculation: two formulations are considered bioequivalent when both the Cmax ratio and the AUC ratio (test/reference, each as a 90% confidence interval in real trials — we compare point estimates here for teaching purposes) fall within 80–125%. tmax is often compared too, but it is not part of the formal criteria — differences in absorption rate alone do not fail a BE study if Cmax and AUC are within range.

Worked Example (hypothetical, for clarity)

Same drug, same dose (100 mg), given IV and orally. IV AUC = 50 mg·h/L. Oral AUC = 35 mg·h/L.

F = AUCoral / AUCIV = 35 / 50 = 0.70 — 70% of the oral dose reaches systemic circulation intact.

Now suppose a generic (test) product of the same drug is compared to the brand (reference), both given at 200 mg: Cmaxtest = 18 mg/L, Cmaxref = 20 mg/L; AUCtest = 88 mg·h/L, AUCref = 95 mg·h/L.

Cmax ratio = 18/20 = 90% (within 80–125% ✓)    AUC ratio = 88/95 = 92.6% (within 80–125% ✓) → Bioequivalent
